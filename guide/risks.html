Risks and blind spots

A planning checklist, not a ranking of failure probabilities. Revisit it when household circumstances, investments or rules change.

Limited review, 20 September 2026: the cited policy and residency statements were checked. Future returns, inflation, crisis access and personal risk probabilities remain unknown.

Sequence-of-returns risk

Withdrawals make the order of returns matter. As a simple illustration, start with €100 and withdraw €10 at each year-end: returns of −20% then +25% leave €77.50; reversing them leaves €82. The same compounded return without withdrawals does not imply the same outcome with withdrawals. This two-year example is not a retirement success-rate estimate.

Consider spending flexibility, asset allocation, accessible reserves and realistic fallback income together. None guarantees that a plan survives; cash can run out, work may be unavailable and essential spending may not be reducible. A projection using a constant return does not establish a probability of success.

Health insurance gap

Use the health-insurance guide to identify coverage gaps for each adult. Model the cost and duration of an actual gap, not a universal extra portfolio amount. Eligibility and future premiums need checking separately.

Relocating abroad can break the tax structure

There are two sides to a move. EMTA's investment-account guidance states that losing Estonian tax residence ends this deferral and requires closure reporting and settlement of the Estonian tax liability. That does not necessarily mean closing the underlying bank or securities account.

The destination may not recognise the Estonian investment account. Its treatment of income, acquisition cost and withdrawals needs separate review; do not assume contributions-first treatment transfers with you.

Depending on the destination, wealth, inheritance and real-estate taxes may also apply. A move therefore requires a new plan under the destination country's law.

Mitigation: before moving, obtain destination-specific tax advice and record account contributions, market values and cost basis. The treatment may differ substantially before and after tax residence changes.

Policy risk

Current rules are not promises for the entire planning horizon. Test whether the plan still works with less favourable tax or benefit assumptions. The planner's rule year and review dates describe loaded information, not future legislation. See Sources & verification for checked rules and open questions.

Concentration

Separate underlying investments from the institution holding them. Local employment, property and business revenue can share economic risks, while a pension fund based in Estonia may invest globally. Look through funds to their holdings rather than counting institutions or flags.

Investor.gov's allocation and diversification guidance describes spreading exposure across and within asset classes. That is distinct from spreading custody across providers. Neither two brokers nor a foreign address proves independent infrastructure or uninterrupted access in a crisis. Review contracts, recovery routes, accessible cash and portable records; see account protection. Crisis access remains unverified, not a guaranteed benefit of foreign custody.

Estonian inflation ≠ eurozone inflation

The ECB's 2% target is for the euro area over the medium term, not a forecast for Estonia or your household's purchases. Test several inflation and spending-growth assumptions, including an adverse case. No fixed 2.5–3% range is established here as sufficient. Keep nominal and real assumptions consistent rather than subtracting inflation twice from an already-real return. See the calculator's assumption explanations.

Under-modelling the bridge

Use each person's actual pension access conditions and chosen payment dates, not universal ages. Pillar III's favourable-tax age also depends on acquisition history; earlier withdrawals are a separate choice. The accessible portfolio must cover the bridge and any gap after finite pension payments end. See pension access and payouts.

Longevity, care, and the boring stuff

Include scenarios for long life, care needs, disability and one partner outliving the other. Costs, income and unpaid household work may all change. Review cover, ownership and estate arrangements alongside portfolio planning; the suitability of insurance and legal documents needs individual advice. This guide does not quantify these probabilities or rank them against withdrawal failure.

The non-financial half

Discuss time, purpose, social contact and each partner's preferences as well as money. Different desired retirement dates are scenarios to model, not evidence that a household plan is doomed.
