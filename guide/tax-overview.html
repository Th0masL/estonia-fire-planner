Estonian tax in one screen

Scope: selected rules for an Estonian tax-resident individual in 2026, with company rules identified separately. Limited source review: 20 September 2026. This is not a complete tax return or a personal tax ruling; future rules and cross-border outcomes are not guaranteed.

Item Rate / treatment Important qualification
Personal income tax 22% standard rate Applies to taxable income, not every receipt. Exemptions, deductions and special pension rates can change the result
Basic exemption €700/month (€8,400/year) From 1 January 2026, no income taper. Pensionable-age exemption is €776/month instead, not an additional allowance. An exemption reduces taxable income; it is not a cash benefit
Capital gains 22% on taxable gains Not on all sale proceeds. Cost basis, eligible losses and exemptions matter; an eligible investment account can defer tax under its own rules
Dividends from Estonian companies Usually no further personal income tax Standard company-level distribution tax applies. Redistribution of historic 14/86-taxed dividends can still require 7% withholding; see the company guide
Foreign dividends Exempt in Estonia if the underlying profit or dividend was taxed abroad Exempt dividends still go in table 8.8. Under ordinary taxation, untaxed foreign dividends go in table 8.1 and are taxable. Investment-account treatment has additional rules; keep evidence
Qualifying residential rent 17.6% of gross rent before personal reliefs 22% on 80% after the automatic 20% deduction. This is not after-cost investment profit. Short-term accommodation and subletting do not qualify; see rental and property tax
Company retained profits Generally not taxed merely when earned Not a blanket exemption for company spending: distributions, fringe benefits, gifts and non-business expenses have their own rules
Company profit distributions Standard 22/78 of the net dividend Equivalent to 22% of the total dividend-plus-tax budget, not 22% of the amount the owner receives. Exempt redistributions and equity repayments need separate analysis
Social tax 33% Employment and board remuneration rules apply; genuine dividends are not salary. The 2026 minimum-obligation base is €886/month (€292.38 tax). Minimum-payment exceptions and health-insurance eligibility are not inferred from this table
Asset ownership Check asset-specific taxes A portfolio balance is not the same as a tax bill, but land and motor-vehicle taxes must not be omitted from spending simply because a broad wealth-tax label says “none”
Inheritances / gifts Receipt and later disposal are different events Inherited property is not taxed on receipt in Estonia; later gains can be taxable. Gifted property, company/employer gifts, foreign taxes and inherited obligations need separate checks
Standard VAT 24% From 1 July 2025; reduced rates and exempt supplies also exist

The rate/exemption/social-tax rows follow EMTA's 2026 rates. Dividend distinctions follow EMTA company distributions and foreign income, “Dividends received abroad”. See EMTA residential rent, estates and standard VAT.

Filing year is not the income year

For 2025 income declared in 2026, the filing deadline was 30 April 2026. The additional-payment and final refund deadline is 1 October 2026. Electronic refunds started 5 March 2026 where no further verification was needed. Foreign income or capital gains do not by themselves establish a separate October-only refund schedule. Check your own decision and outstanding requests.

These dates do not make the 2026 €700 exemption applicable to 2025 income. Use the rules for the income year and confirm the relevant filing calendar. Sources: EMTA foreign-income declaration deadline and 2026 refund/payment timetable.

What this means for a FIRE plan

Compare spendable, after-tax cash flows, not headline rates alone. Tax deferral is not tax exemption. Keep supporting records, distinguish personal assets from company assets, and budget for health insurance as well as property, investment and household costs.

Still case-specific: residency and treaties, evidence of foreign tax, eligible assets/losses, gifts and inheritances, payroll exceptions and final tax/refund amounts.
