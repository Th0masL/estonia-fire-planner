Account protection and systemic risk

Checked 20 September 2026: the limited rules below are supported by the linked authorities. They do not certify a particular account's eligibility, custody chain or accessibility during a crisis.

1. Multiple investment accounts — how it actually works

EMTA's investment-account guidance, “How are taxable gains calculated” and declaration sections, allows several accounts and calculates contributions/payments across them in date order. It is not simply a year-end netting exercise.

Transfers between the taxpayer's own investment accounts do not create a fresh contribution or a taxable payment. This assumes qualifying accounts and correct treatment of the transfer, not merely two accounts carrying the same app label.

Declare account details and relevant movements in the annual return, table 6.5. Keep records from the start and review reports before submission. A provider's report cannot establish what happened at another institution. Do not confuse preparing an account correctly before investing with a requirement to file the annual declaration before the first trade.

Open review: account-specific foreign-provider eligibility, correction of old filings, foreign-currency reporting and provider workflows. Follow the investment-account guide and obtain clarification where the actual arrangement is uncertain. Transfers can have commercial costs even when their tax treatment is neutral.

2. Deposit guarantees and investor compensation

Protection What it addresses What it does not promise
Deposit guarantee Eligible bank deposits within the applicable limit Unlimited cover or uninterrupted access
Client-asset safeguarding Arrangements protecting ownership of client securities Stable investment value or an immediate transfer after failure
Investor compensation Eligible claims when an institution cannot return client assets Compensation for ordinary market losses

Eligible bank deposits

Estonia's ordinary limit is €100,000 per depositor per credit institution, including accrued interest across accounts. Different brands or branches are not automatically separate institutions.

Tagatisfond describes a seven-working-day compensation deadline after suspension, subject to its conditions; this is not continuous access. Joint holders are treated as depositors for their shares, alongside their other deposits at that institution.

An additional €100,000 may apply to qualifying personal receipts credited within the preceding 6 months, including specified residential-property transactions and life events. Ordinary savings for a future purchase do not qualify merely because of their size.

Source: Tagatisfond depositor FAQ, limit, payment, joint-deposit and temporary-balance sections. Confirm exclusions, ownership, evidence and the applicable scheme with the institution. Foreign branches and broker-held cash require account-specific checks.

Securities and missing assets

MiFID II Article 16(8)–(9), published by ESMA requires arrangements safeguarding client ownership and funds, including in insolvency. This legal duty is not evidence that every provider's records and custody arrangements will work without a shortfall or delay.

The European Commission's explanation distinguishes failure to return assets from investment losses. A fall in an ETF's price is not an investor-compensation claim.

Tagatisfond's investor FAQ states an Estonian compensation limit of €20,000 for eligible investments, with exclusions. The limit applies per investor at an institution, not per security. It covers qualifying obligations to return money or securities, not a promise that a portfolio can never lose more than that sum. The investor process has its own timetable and may take months; do not apply the deposit scheme's seven-working-day rule to it.

There are two distinct levels: your claim for fund units held through an intermediary, and the assets owned within the fund. The FAQ lists fund units among covered instruments but excludes investment fund assets themselves. Do not read that exclusion as either “ETF units can never be covered” or “everything inside a fund is guaranteed”.

Example: a temporarily large cash balance

For an eligible ordinary deposit of €175,000, ignoring interest and other balances, the standard limit leaves €75,000 above the ordinary cover. This is an uncovered amount, not a prediction of loss. A qualifying temporary balance could change the result; evidence of the source and dates is essential.

Check legal institutions and guarantee headroom before splitting deposits. Moving the money into an ETF changes the risk rather than increasing the deposit guarantee. Use the purchase-reserve checklist for money needed on a particular date.

3. Planning for disrupted access

Ownership, compensation and access are separate questions. A second provider may offer another operational route, but this is a planning consideration—not a verified guarantee against capital controls, outages or shared custodians.

Before relying on a second account:

  • Identify the contracting entity, regulator and relevant compensation scheme.
  • Ask where client cash and securities are held, including nominee or subcustodian arrangements and any securities-lending or collateral terms.
  • Check transfer-out procedures, records, fees and account-recovery options.
  • Keep recent statements and ownership records securely accessible.
  • Keep essential near-term spending available without depending on a market sale or a compensation claim.

A broker's registered country does not describe the entire custody chain. Foreign assets do not establish immunity from restrictions affecting residents. Nor does global equity guarantee a positive euro return in a eurozone crisis: currency changes do not eliminate equity losses.

Still unverified: provider-specific custody and protection, foreign compensation percentages/limits, future capital controls, actual recovery timelines and the precise resolution loss hierarchy. Historical Greece/Cyprus figures and macro-risk rankings have been removed rather than used as predictions for Estonia. There is no universal account count or portfolio threshold that solves these risks.
