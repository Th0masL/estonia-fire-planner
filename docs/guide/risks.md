# Risks and blind spots

Short list of what actually kills plans. Not exhaustive — a checklist to revisit once the real numbers are in.

## Sequence-of-returns risk
A bad first decade of withdrawals does permanent damage. Mitigation is flexibility, a cash buffer, and any earned income — not clever allocation. See [fire-basics.md](fire-basics.md#sequence-of-returns-risk).

## Health insurance gap
Covered in detail in the [health-insurance guide](health-insurance.md). Listed again because it is commonly omitted from Estonian FIRE plans and can add roughly €100k or more to an FI target.

## Relocating abroad can break the tax structure

One major structural risk is that **another country may not recognise the Estonian investment account.** After a change of tax residence, the destination jurisdiction may apply its own rules to investment income, cost basis and withdrawals; Estonian deferral and contributions-first treatment should not be assumed to survive.

Depending on the destination, wealth, inheritance and real-estate taxes may also apply. A move therefore requires a new plan under the destination country's law.

**Mitigation:** before moving, obtain destination-specific tax advice and record account contributions, market values and cost basis. The treatment may differ substantially before and after tax residence changes.

## Policy risk
Estonia changed income tax, VAT and the basic exemption three times in three years. The investment account regime and 0% corporate retained-earnings tax are both politically load-bearing and both have been publicly debated. A plan that only works if these survive 20 years unchanged is fragile. Don't over-optimise around any single rule.

## Concentration
Estonian salary + Estonian property + Estonian bank + Estonian pension + Estonia-based business revenue can become **one bet placed five times**. These exposures may correlate in exactly the scenario where diversification is most valuable.

Related and specific to the region: **custody location**. Assets held at an Estonian institution, in Estonia, denominated in EUR, is fine under normal conditions. Given the border, it's worth a deliberate decision — not paranoia, just diversifying custody (e.g. some assets at a non-Estonian broker) and keeping documentation portable. Cheap insurance; decide once and move on.

## Estonian inflation ≠ eurozone inflation
Estonia ran ~19–20% inflation in 2022 against a eurozone average of ~8–9%. Small catching-up economies inside a currency union diverge. Planning at ECB's 2% understates your real withdrawal needs — use 2.5–3%.

## Under-modelling the bridge
Pillar II and III unlock at 63–65 and 60. Everything before that comes from the IK. The bridge is often the binding constraint, and it's easy to look "on track" in aggregate while the bridge is short.

## Longevity, care, and the boring stuff
Long-term care costs, disability, and one partner outliving the other by 15 years are all more likely than a market scenario that breaks a 3.5% SWR. Insurance and legal structure (wills, matrimonial regime) do more here than portfolio design.

## The non-financial half
The failure mode nobody plans for: reaching FI and finding out the job was providing structure, status and social contact. Also — FIRE is a **household** plan. If one partner is enthusiastic and the other isn't, the plan is already broken regardless of the spreadsheet.
