# Property and rental income

Property can provide rental cash flow, but the tax calculation and the cash
available for FIRE spending are different quantities.

> **Limited review, 20 September 2026:** the cited general tax rules were checked. Examples are conditional, not a completed tax return or a property recommendation.

## Residential rent: when the 20% deduction applies

[EMTA's rental-income guidance](https://www.emta.ee/en/private-client/taxes-and-payment/taxable-income/income-lease-and-rent-licence-fees)
allows an individual a 20% deduction for qualifying residential letting, without
expense receipts for that deduction. It is not a blanket allowance for every
property activity: accommodation services and subletting do not qualify.

Declare the relevant gross rental income; the deduction is applied on the return.
EMTA distinguishes utility reimbursements from rent, while a tenant's payments
of the owner's loan instalments or land tax count as rental income. Classification
depends on the actual arrangement. Annual income and permitted deductions affect
the final tax bill.

**Illustration:** €12,000 qualifying annual rent × 80% = €9,600 taxable rental
income. At an assumed 22%, before other personal relief, that gives **€2,112 tax**,
or **17.6% of gross rent**, leaving €9,888 before actual property expenses and
loan payments. The 20% deduction is not cash reimbursed to the landlord.
See the [dated tax-rate evidence](verification.md#income-tax-and-basic-exemption).

**Source caveat:** EMTA's rental page still contains examples using a 20% tax
rate. Those examples are not evidence for today's rate; the 20% rental deduction
and the personal income-tax rate are separate inputs.

## What to enter in the simulator

The **Rental & other net income** field accepts an amount you supply; it does not
calculate rental tax, vacancy or property expenses from gross rent. Budget for
tax, repairs, management, insurance, land tax, vacancy and financing cash flows
before deciding how much recurring income is available. Account for each cost
once: do not subtract a loan payment here and also enter it as household spending.

Stress-test periods without rent and exceptional repairs. A steady monthly input
is a planning simplification, not a forecast of uninterrupted occupancy.

## Selling a home: exemption versus taxable gains

[EMTA's residence-sale guidance](https://www.emta.ee/en/private-client/taxes-and-payment/taxable-income/transfer-immovable-property/transfer-place-residence)
requires actual residence use until transfer; a registered address alone does not
establish it. The residence exemption applies to one sale within two years,
counting from the day after the sale's land-register entry. Each owner's use and
circumstances matter; mixed use can restrict the exemption.

If an exemption does not apply, **taxable gains are not the entire sale price**.
[EMTA's gains calculation](https://www.emta.ee/en/private-client/taxes-and-payment/taxable-income/transfer-immovable-property/calculation-taxable-gains)
deducts qualifying acquisition costs and expenses directly related to sale.
Keep supporting documents; do not assume all expenditure qualifies or that every
second sale has identical treatment.

## Ownership, costs and concentration

- **Via an OÜ:** compare cash reaching the owner after costs and extraction, not just retained company income. The [company guide](company.md) keeps rental-specific questions open.
- **Land tax and finance:** obtain the plot-specific assessment and actual loan terms. The [home-purchase guide](property-purchase.md) distinguishes personal mortgage rules from provider offers.
- **Exposure:** local employment and property can share local economic risks. A pension fund or broker based in Estonia need not hold mainly Estonian assets. Inspect underlying holdings separately from custody, jurisdiction and liquidity risks; provider location alone does not measure correlation.

**Still unverified:** individual rental eligibility, business/VAT treatment,
company-property comparisons, foreign property, local land-tax relief and rates,
mixed-use allocations and specific sale exemptions. This guide does not establish
that property is more attractive in Estonia than elsewhere in Europe.
